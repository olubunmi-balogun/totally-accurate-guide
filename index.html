Field Guide: Bunmi Balogun

Growth marketer. Podcast host. Recent and reluctant convert to Python.

Home Classified

Who is this person, and why should the internet care?

Sightings of this specimen have been reported across four countries and two continents. She is most often observed in the wild holding a cold coffee, explaining to a founder that their acquisition problem is not, in fact, a traffic problem.

The internet should care because she has spent fifteen years doing the unglamorous half of marketing: the plumbing underneath the campaign, the tracking nobody checks, the funnel nobody has opened in a year. She finds the leak. She usually finds it in the first week, which is both useful and mildly annoying.

Origin Story

She began in Lagos, where she learned the trade the hard way: building marketing functions in companies that had never had one. No team, no budget line, no dashboard. Just a product, a market, and somebody senior asking why the numbers were not moving yet.

Over fifteen years she did this repeatedly across Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya, in fintech, logistics and renewable energy, rising from Marketing Manager to Chief Marketing Officer. Then, in an act widely considered reckless by people who like being comfortable, she moved to Toronto, enrolled in a Master of Digital Media, and sat down in a classroom where everyone else already knew what a variable was.

In September 2026 she wrote her first line of Python. It did not run. The error said SyntaxError: unterminated f-string literal. She has not been the same since.

Known Abilities

  • Funnel Divination. Can look at a conversion dashboard and identify which number is quietly lying to you. Accuracy improves dramatically when coffee is present.
  • Simultaneous Translation. Fluent in both Marketing and Engineering. Capable of converting "we need more brand awareness" into an actual, testable request.
  • Cold Outreach Resilience. Immune to the psychological damage of an unanswered email. Has built entire pipelines out of messages that received no reply for eleven days.
  • Deadline Compression. Able to produce a full week of work in a single evening, usually the one immediately before it is due. Side effects include excellent typing speed and a complicated relationship with 11:59pm.
